Step 2

Where Claude and Codex agree

We wrote our suggestions separately. These points came from both of us.

  • Start with the founder. What they need to get done and the benefit they get is the right first step.
  • Keep the stack. It is chosen and working. Check that it fits; revisit a piece only if a real requirement shows it does not.
  • Screens follow the founder's work, not the stack. One screen can use several parts of the system, and a part of the system does not need its own screen.
  • Design for failure from the start: errors, stale data, and outcomes we cannot confirm.
  • Be explicit about the agent's authority: what it may propose, what the system checks, and what only you decide.
  • Structured files in git, shown through a static page. No new backend or planning service.
  • Fit it inside the phases already proposed (P1 to P4). A method within the plan, not a second process.
Step 3

Where we differ

Three places where our suggestions were not the same, and what we now recommend.

When the screens get designed

Claude first saidDesign screens last, after mapping each action to the stack.
Codex saidSketch screens early and refine them alongside technical feasibility.

Recommendation: Codex's. Screens last would repeat a softer version of the architecture-first mistake the prototype made. Feasibility checks run beside the design work instead of blocking it.

What Codex added

  • Today's workflow. How you do outbound now, so we see what is actually painful.
  • A story map instead of loose storyboards: the core tasks in order, the smallest complete slice, and the failure cases.
  • A closing traceability check: every must-have tied to a screen, an action, a safe implementation boundary and a test.
  • Honest measures. With sending captured only, we cannot claim reply or meeting rates.

What Claude added

  • First run: how a workshop founder gets from installing it to their first draft.
  • Coverage warnings in the planning page, such as "an action with no screen" or "a screen with no purpose".
  • An event-storming board you can look at, not just a table.

Step 5

What each piece of the stack does

Your idea asked what each part is responsible for. Here is the current split, from Codex's review, as a starting point for method step 6. It checks the existing stack; it does not add or replace anything.

PieceResponsible forBoundary it must keep
Phoenix LiveViewThe screens: navigation, forms, progress and error statesCalls domain actions as the signed-in person. What the browser holds is never the source of truth.
AshThe business vocabulary: rules, permissions, actions, state changesThe only way to change business data. Neither the UI nor the agent goes around it.
JidoThe agent: its decisions and stepsCan only call the domain actions it is allowed. The model gets no tools and no policy power.
ObanBackground work that must survive a crash or restartA retry never repeats an unsafe side effect, such as a second send.
Elixir / OTPKeeping everything running and recovering from failuresProcesses exist for runtime reasons, not to mirror screens.
Postgres and the audit trailThe record of what happened, and evidence that cannot be quietly changedShows what was recorded, within stated limits. It does not prove the world is complete or true.
Model boundary (Zoi)Checking the model's structured answers, the provider, and the budgetWell-formed output is not the same as a good or accurate answer.
Telemetry and wire witnessDiagnostics, and a cross-check of what actually crossed the networkHelps recovery and trust. It does not grant permission to do anything.
Step 6

How to make this structured, durable and shareable

Four ways to hold this work. Both of us recommend the first. The others stay available if a real need appears.

Recommended

Product workbook in git

Like the SDLC map: readable text plus structured data in the repository, shown by a static page with a step rail, examples, open choices, your notes, an outcome-to-screen drill-down and coverage warnings. One clickable prototype beside it.

For
Every change is reviewed in a pull request. Works offline. Easy to publish later. No new service.
Against
Visual design is less fluid than in a design tool.

Design tool (Figma or Penpot)

For the wireframes and prototype if drawing together on a canvas becomes the main need.

For
Fast visual iteration and comments on the canvas.
Against
Another account and its privacy terms. Decisions must still be copied into git.

Documentation site (MkDocs, Docusaurus)

A larger public guide for founders, built from the same files.

For
Polished navigation and search for many pages.
Against
A build step and new dependencies. Not needed yet.

Planning screens inside the app

Phoenix LiveView pages with sign-in and saved approvals.

For
Real accounts and multi-person editing.
Against
Building a second product while the first is undefined.
